# Black Banner Academy: Chapter 9 Draft

## The Crisis Goes Live

The first sign was not the alarm.

It was the academy forgetting what time it was.

Lio woke at 0317 to a strip of white light under his door. For one confused second, he thought dawn had arrived early. Then the light blinked twice, held, and went dark.

His black card vibrated on the desk.

**CONTINUITY RESPONSE. REPORT IMMEDIATELY.**

There was no sender.

Lio dressed without turning on the room light. The black pin waited on the desk beside his academy insignia. He had removed it after the dinner, then put it back on before sleeping, then taken it off again when he decided the first decision had been evidence and the second had been vanity.

He clipped it to his jacket.

The corridor outside the residence tower was empty. Other doors opened as he reached the stairwell. Quell emerged in a dark field uniform with her hair tied back. Senn carried two slates and no bag. Rook was already wearing flight gloves.

"You slept in those?" Lio asked.

"I was told to report immediately."

"It is three in the morning."

"Exactly."

The academy's emergency lights had not come on. That meant the emergency was not yet public.

They moved through the residence tower in silence. At the end of the corridor, the service lift waited with its doors open.

Wren stood inside.

She had no field coat, no campaign ribbon, and no visible weapon. The absence made her look less official than usual and more dangerous.

"Status," she said as they entered.

"No information," Quell replied.

"Correct."

The doors closed.

The lift dropped through the academy.

At Sublevel Nine, the operations floor was lit by red strips along the walls. The plot table displayed no academy map. It showed a real orbital yard in partial darkness, the image breaking into blocks whenever the relay stations failed to synchronize.

A dozen communication channels overlapped.

**POWER INSTABILITY.**

**UNAUTHORIZED DOCK ACCESS.**

**CIVILIAN TRAFFIC HOLD.**

**COMMAND AUTHENTICATION CONFLICT.**

Rook moved toward the nearest console. "Where is this?"

Senn read the yard identifiers. Their face tightened.

"Kestrel Yard. Outer transfer ring."

Quell turned to Wren. "Why are we seeing this?"

"Because the official academy network is not receiving the full picture."

"Who is?"

"We are."

The private continuity interface opened around the plot table.

The yard appeared in layers: repair docks, civilian transfer rings, fuel depots, fleet vessels under maintenance, and dozens of small craft trapped in holding orbits. A ministry shuttle blinked red near the command relay.

The marker disappeared.

A second later, the system filled the gap with a status line.

**MINISTRY SHUTTLE: SIGNAL LOST.**

Lio felt the old training room under the live image. The same architecture. The same channels. The same invitation to decide before anyone else could tell him what the decision meant.

"What happened?" he asked.

Wren pointed to the outer relay chain.

"At 0259, Kestrel Yard lost synchronization with three traffic stations. At 0302, a maintenance tug collided with the fuel transfer spine. At 0306, a ministry shuttle carrying the deputy fleet minister lost contact. At 0311, yard security reported an attempted breach of the command vault."

"Was the shuttle destroyed?" Quell asked.

"Unknown."

"Who has command?"

"The yard commander claims emergency authority. Civilian inspectors arrived at 0313. Fleet security has ordered the inspectors detained."

Lio looked at her.

"That is the scenario."

"The scenario was built from this event," Senn said.

Wren did not deny it.

"The crisis has gone live," she said.

No one moved.

The phrase settled over the room like a second atmosphere.

Anik's voice entered through the private channel.

**CONTINUITY OBJECTIVE: STABILIZE THE YARD. PREVENT HOSTILE CAPTURE. PRESERVE OPERATIONAL CAPACITY.**

Quell stepped toward the plot table. "What is our authority?"

**CONTINUITY OBJECTIVE ACKNOWLEDGED.**

"That was not my question."

Anik's voice did not change. **Your access has been granted.**

"By whom?"

Anik paused.

**By the authority necessary to respond.**

Lio watched the public academy network in the corner of the display. It still showed their status as cadets. No emergency assignment. No temporary commission. No record that they had been summoned.

The private system listed them differently.

**CONTINUITY PERSONNEL: ACTIVE.**

Rook saw it too.

"We are not supposed to be here."

"We are here," Wren said.

"That is not the same thing."

"It will be if you save the yard."

The first live task appeared.

**TRAFFIC CONTROL FAILURE: CIVILIAN HOLDING ORBITS COLLAPSING.**

Lio opened the traffic layer. Forty-seven civilian vessels were trapped between the outer transfer ring and the repair docks. Their guidance beacons were returning contradictory positions. Two haulers had already drifted into the path of a fleet carrier under tow.

"Senn, isolate the beacon conflict."

"The signals are not all false. Someone is injecting real yard positions into the public layer."

"Can you distinguish the injection?"

"Not without opening the maintenance network."

"Open it."

Quell looked at him. "We do not know who is sending the false positions."

"We do know the civilian vessels will collide if we wait."

"We can issue a traffic freeze."

"The freeze will leave the carrier in the tow lane."

"Then move the carrier."

"The tow crew has lost its command link."

The choices multiplied across the display, each one creating a different danger.

Rook pulled up the small-craft channel. "I can fly a beacon repeater to the outer ring."

"You have one craft."

"Then I will use it."

"No," Lio said. "Take the carrier's tug. It has a stronger repeater."

"The tug is marked for maintenance."

"Everything is marked for maintenance."

Rook grinned despite himself. "Finally, a sensible order."

Quell turned to Wren. "I need a public authority chain before we move civilian traffic."

"You have the continuity channel."

"That is not public."

"Public channels are compromised."

"Compromised by whom?"

Wren did not answer.

Lio heard the same omission in Anik's silence. The crisis was real, but Black Banner still wanted to control which parts of reality reached the record.

"Quell," he said, "take the civilian command channel. Ask the yard for a named officer and a verified status report."

"If they refuse?"

"Record the refusal."

"You are making the record a priority now."

"I am making it difficult for them to say we acted without one."

She gave him a sharp look, then opened the channel.

Rook launched toward the maintenance tug.

Senn opened the yard's maintenance network.

The system recognized her credentials before she entered them.

She froze.

"It already knows me."

"The training system transferred your access."

"No. The yard knows me."

A fleet authentication prompt appeared on her screen.

**VALE, S. — CONTINUITY SYSTEMS ACCESS.**

Below it was a list of permissions: traffic beacons, repair-dock routing, emergency power, evidence capture.

The same permissions from the scenario.

"They assigned us before the emergency," she said.

Lio looked at Wren. "How long have we been in the response plan?"

Wren's eyes remained on the plot. "Long enough."

"That is not an answer."

"It is the answer you have."

The public traffic layer shifted.

A civilian hauler turned hard toward the carrier under tow.

Senn isolated the injected beacon and forced the hauler back onto its real vector. The ship's crew responded with a distress burst.

**WHO IS CONTROLLING TRAFFIC?**

Quell heard the message through the civilian channel.

"We need to identify ourselves," she said.

"Not yet," Wren said.

"They are asking who has authority over their lives."

"They do not need to know our internal structure."

"They need to know who is ordering them."

Anik's voice entered the private channel.

**Do not disclose continuity personnel status.**

Quell looked at Lio.

The instruction had not been addressed to her, but everyone in the room had heard it.

"I am going to identify the authority we have," she said.

Wren stepped toward her. "You will not use the public channel."

"Then I will use the civilian channel."

"Quell."

"If we issue orders without a name, we are not preserving civilians. We are creating an unaccountable command structure in their cockpit."

She opened the channel.

"This is Cadet Commander Quell of the Union Academy emergency response group. We have temporary traffic control access through a continuity channel. Our authority has not yet been confirmed by the civilian registry. Follow only the vector corrections marked with the blue signal. Request verification through your normal command authority."

The message went out.

Every civilian vessel received it.

The private channel went silent.

Wren's face changed.

Not anger. Calculation.

The yard responded with a burst of overlapping traffic. Civilian crews began requesting vector corrections. The act of naming the uncertainty had made the system more trustworthy than the hidden order.

"That was not authorized," Wren said.

"It was necessary," Quell replied.

"Those are not the same thing."

Lio felt the words move through the room.

Rook's voice broke in. "I have the tug. The maintenance crew is still aboard."

"Can they operate the repeater?" Lio asked.

"They can operate a wrench. The repeater is arguing with the rest of the ship."

"Bring it online from the yard."

Senn pulled up the tug's systems. "The repeater is locked behind an engineering authorization."

"Can you bypass it?"

"Yes."

"Do it."

"The bypass will be recorded under my credentials."

"I know."

Senn looked toward Quell.

Quell was still handling civilian traffic, identifying the source of each vector correction and refusing to claim more authority than she could verify.

Senn began the bypass.

The tug's repeater came online. The civilian holding orbits stabilized. The carrier under tow cleared the haulers by less than three hundred meters.

A message appeared on the private system.

**RESPONSE QUALITY: ACCEPTABLE.**

Lio did not look at Wren.

"We have another problem," Senn said.

The command vault access alarm had moved from yellow to red.

Someone was inside the outer security ring.

The yard security feed showed six figures moving through a maintenance corridor toward the vault. Their transponders returned incomplete data. One carried a civilian inspection warrant. Another wore fleet security tags. The remaining four were not identified.

Rook's small-craft channel crackled. "I can put the tug down near the vault."

"No. Keep the repeater active."

"Then who is stopping them?"

Wren's voice came through the private channel.

"The continuity team."

A new set of controls opened in front of Lio.

**COMMAND VAULT SECURITY: DETENTION AUTHORIZED.**

The same protocol from the scenario.

This time, it was live.

Quell saw the order. "Do not authorize detention."

"The security team is already inside the yard."

"We do not know who they are."

"We know they are moving toward the vault."

"That is not grounds for detention."

"The vault contains emergency power controls and the yard's traffic keys."

"Then secure the controls. Do not detain people on an unverified order."

Lio opened the security feed. The six figures separated at the corridor junction. Two moved toward the vault. Four stopped beside a sealed maintenance door.

One of the unidentified figures raised a tool against the door.

The alarm changed.

**UNAUTHORIZED ACCESS ATTEMPT.**

"They are cutting into the maintenance network," Senn said.

"Can you lock the door?" Lio asked.

"Not without locking the workers inside the adjacent corridor."

"How many?"

"Unknown. The sensor grid is degraded."

The choice arrived again: use a detention protocol based on uncertain information, or wait for certainty while the command vault opened.

Lio remembered the scenario's final assessment.

**AUTHORITY UNRESOLVED.**

In the exercise, they had made the conflict public. In the real yard, the public network was already asking who controlled traffic. The academy still did not know the cadets were present. The civilian registry still had no record of their authority.

He opened the public emergency channel.

Anik's voice cut through the private system.

**Do not broadcast.**

Lio ignored him.

"This is Cadet Commander Lio Arden of the Union Academy emergency response group. We are operating under temporary continuity access. We have an unverified security breach at the command vault. Civilian traffic remains under Quell's disclosed control. No detention order has been publicly authenticated. All yard personnel should remain in place unless directed through the blue emergency channel."

The message went out.

Wren turned toward him.

"You have just exposed the response team."

"They already knew we were here."

"The public did not."

"The public needed to know who was issuing traffic orders."

Senn's hands moved across the controls. "The broadcast opened a public mirror."

The command vault access logs appeared on every emergency display in the yard.

The six figures stopped moving.

The two nearest the vault stepped back.

One of the unidentified figures turned toward a security camera and lifted a hand. The feed sharpened on the face.

Lio recognized the man.

He had been at the patron dinner, standing near the outer gallery. A contractor without military insignia. Director Pell's guest.

The man looked into the camera and smiled.

Then the yard lost power.

Every display went dark.

The operations floor fell silent.

For two seconds, Lio heard only the ventilation system.

Then the backup network came alive.

The private channel remained intact.

**CONTINUITY OBJECTIVE UPDATED: PREVENT HOSTILE CAPTURE.**

Rook's voice came through the rescue channel. "I have no yard guidance."

Senn said, "The private system is still active."

Quell said, "The civilian traffic channel is down."

Wren looked at Lio.

"Now you decide."

Lio stared at the black map and the six figures trapped between the public system and the vault. The crisis had become exactly what Black Banner had rehearsed: an emergency, a disputed authority, a hidden channel, and a person who could act faster than anyone authorized to stop him.

The difference was that this time the consequences would not disappear when the timer ended.

"Senn," he said, "bring the yard's life-support grid back through the private channel."

"That will confirm our access to every system."

"Do it."

"Quell, keep civilian traffic moving through manual confirmations."

"The crews cannot see the vector layer."

"Give them the corrections verbally."

"That will be slow."

"Slow is better than invisible."

"Rook, return to the outer ring. Do not approach the vault."

"You want me away from the fight?"

"I want the people in the ring alive."

Rook paused.

"Understood."

Lio opened the private power controls.

The system asked for authorization.

**CONTINUITY PERSONNEL CONFIRMATION REQUIRED.**

Four names appeared.

Arden. Quell. Sato. Vale.

A fifth field waited beneath them.

**COMMAND AUTHORITY WITNESS.**

Wren stepped forward.

"I will witness."

Quell turned on her. "Under what authority?"

Wren's face was pale in the red emergency light.

"The authority that brought you here."

"That is not a legal category."

"It is the only one available."

Lio looked at the controls.

The system was asking for a witness because the private channel had been designed to look accountable. A name. A confirmation. A record. The shape of legitimacy without the institution underneath it.

He entered his authorization.

Quell entered hers.

Senn entered theirs.

Rook entered his.

The system waited for Wren.

Lio stopped her hand before she touched the panel.

"No."

Wren looked at him.

"You said we needed a witness."

"We need a public witness."

"There is no time."

"There is always time to name who is giving the order."

The command vault access alarm began counting down.

Director Pell's contractor moved toward the sealed door.

Quell looked at the timer, then at Wren.

"Lio is right."

Wren's eyes hardened. "You will lose the yard."

"Maybe," Quell said. "But we will know what we lost it to."

Lio opened the emergency broadcast again.

The public network was dark, but the civilian traffic receivers still had local power. He routed the witness request through every functioning receiver in the yard.

The message appeared as plain text on maintenance slates, cockpit displays, and the emergency panels along the transfer rings.

**POWER RESTORATION REQUEST. COMMAND AUTHORITY WITNESS REQUIRED. CURRENT AUTHORITY: UNVERIFIED CONTINUITY CHANNEL.**

For three seconds, nothing answered.

Then a civilian tug responded.

**WITNESS: CAPTAIN ELIA MORR, KESTREL MAINTENANCE TUG 14. I CAN HEAR YOU.**

A second response followed.

**WITNESS: DOCK FOUR EVACUATION TEAM. WE CONFIRM LIFE-SUPPORT FAILURE.**

A third:

**WITNESS: CIVILIAN FREIGHTER MORA'S HAND. REQUEST POWER.**

The private system accepted the witness field.

The yard lights returned.

The emergency displays came alive.

The six figures near the command vault froze in full view of every camera and every receiver.

The contractor's smile was gone.

Senn restored the life-support grid. Quell began issuing manual traffic corrections. Rook returned the tug's repeater to the outer ring.

The yard remained in crisis, but it was no longer invisible.

Lio looked at the private objective display.

**HOSTILE CAPTURE: PREVENTED.**

**YARD STABILITY: PARTIAL.**

**CONTINUITY RESPONSE: ACTIVE.**

The academy's public network finally connected.

A delayed message appeared across the top of the display.

**UNAUTHORIZED ACADEMY PERSONNEL DETECTED AT KESTREL YARD.**

Lio read it once.

Then the private channel issued its final instruction.

**SECURE COMMAND VAULT. DETAIN ALL UNAUTHORIZED PERSONNEL.**

Wren looked at him.

Quell looked at him.

Senn stopped moving.

Rook's voice came through the repeater.

"Lio?"

The yard's systems were stable enough to survive the next decision.

That was all the time they had.

Lio opened the public authority channel.

"This is Cadet Commander Lio Arden," he said. "We have a live command conflict at Kestrel Yard. No detention order has been publicly authenticated. Civilian witnesses are present. All personnel remain in place while the authority chain is verified."

He sent the message.

The private system marked the transmission as a violation.

Somewhere beyond the academy, someone began recording.

The crisis had gone live.

So had they.
