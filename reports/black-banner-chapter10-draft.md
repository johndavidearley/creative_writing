# Black Banner Academy: Chapter 10 Draft

## Who Authorized This?

The public broadcast did not calm the yard.

It gave everyone a different person to blame.

The civilian traffic channel filled first. Captains demanded to know why cadets were controlling vectors. Dock workers asked whether the command vault had been breached. A repair crew from Dock Four reported that the life-support system had come back under an unauthorized power signature.

Then the fleet security channel opened.

**UNAUTHORIZED ACADEMY PERSONNEL: STAND DOWN.**

The message appeared on every display.

A second line followed.

**YARD COMMAND AUTHORITY CONFIRMED. ALL CONTINUITY PERSONNEL ARE TO BE DETAINED.**

Rook's voice came through the outer-ring repeater. "They are calling us hostile."

Quell remained at the civilian console, one hand pressed against the receiver. "The public channel is asking whether the command authority has been verified."

"What did you tell them?" Lio asked.

"That it has not."

"Good."

"Some of them are asking whether we caused the sabotage."

"What did you tell them?"

Quell looked at him. "That we do not know."

The answer moved through the room with less force than a denial would have. It was honest. It was also dangerous. In a crisis, uncertainty did not remain empty for long. Someone always filled it.

Senn pulled the command vault feed onto the central display.

The six figures had split into three groups. The contractor from the dinner stood near the sealed vault door with one of the fleet security officers. Two civilian inspectors had moved toward the maintenance corridor. The remaining figures had disappeared into the lower yard.

"The contractor is not on the fleet roster," Senn said. "His access tag is private infrastructure."

"Pell's company?" Lio asked.

"I am checking."

"Do not use the public system."

"I am not."

A new message appeared on Senn's console.

**VALE, S. — SYSTEM INTEGRITY BREACH CONFIRMED.**

Her face tightened.

"They are logging every action under our names."

"We know."

"No. We know the public network is logging us. The private channel has generated a personnel record. It has assigned a violation category, an incident score, and a recommendation."

"What recommendation?"

She hesitated.

"Command suitability review."

Rook laughed through the repeater. "That is a polite way to say they are going to ruin us."

Quell looked at Lio. "The civilian inspectors are requesting access to the vault."

The inspector channel opened.

A woman's voice entered the operations floor, strained but controlled.

"This is Inspector Mara Vennett, civilian authority review group. We have a valid inspection warrant and are requesting command-vault access. We are not requesting detention. We are requesting preservation of evidence."

Quell reached for the channel.

"Inspector Vennett, this is Cadet Commander Quell of the Union Academy emergency response group. Your warrant is visible, but the civil registry has not recognized the succession event."

"We know. The yard commander has refused to enter it."

"Can you verify the commander's identity?"

"We can verify his commission. We cannot verify his emergency succession authority."

The fleet security channel cut in.

**INSPECTOR GROUP IS COMPROMISED. DO NOT PROVIDE ACCESS.**

Inspector Vennett's voice sharpened. "That statement is not attached to a named authority."

**YARD COMMAND AUTHORITY CONFIRMED.**

"By whom?" Quell demanded.

The channel closed.

For a moment, only the yard's failing systems answered.

Lio opened the command map.

"Rook, move the maintenance tug to the command-vault perimeter. Do not enter the vault."

"I thought you wanted me on the outer ring."

"I need you to keep the security team from taking the door."

"That sounds like entering the fight."

"It is not."

"It is exactly entering the fight."

Lio watched the vault feed. The contractor had connected a private tool to the security panel. The panel showed a sequence of code injections, each one failing against the yard's temporary lock.

"If they open the vault, the yard's traffic keys are gone."

"Then I will stop them."

"You will keep the perimeter visible."

Rook's response was silence.

Then: "Understood."

His craft altered course.

Quell turned to Lio. "You gave him an order without civilian authority."

"He is operating under the private continuity channel."

"That is not an authority. It is a system permission."

"It is the only one responding."

"Then we need to stop using it as though that makes it valid."

Senn brought up the incident record.

"Quell is right. The private channel has no issuing officer, no public registry, and no chain of appeal. It is an access structure pretending to be command."

"Can you disable it?" Lio asked.

Senn looked at him. "Yes."

"Can we keep the yard alive without it?"

"Not with our current access."

"Then not yet."

Quell's eyes hardened. "That is the argument they are giving you. The system works, so the authority must be acceptable."

"The system is keeping life support online."

"And it is telling us to detain people under an unverified order."

"We have not obeyed that order."

"You broadcasted instead."

"It worked."

"For now."

The phrase stayed between them.

Lio looked at the command vault feed. Director Pell's contractor had stopped trying to open the door. He was speaking to someone through a private comm unit.

Senn enlarged the audio.

The contractor's voice came through in fragments.

"...no, the cadet is not responding to the detention prompt... the broadcast has given the inspectors a witness layer... yes, the yard is still under continuity control..."

Lio looked toward Wren.

"Who is he speaking to?"

Wren stood apart from the consoles, listening.

"I don't know."

"You recognize the protocol."

"I recognize the system."

"You trained us to use it."

"I trained you to understand what it could do."

"You trained us to make hidden authority look operational."

Wren flinched.

It was small, but Lio saw it.

The contractor's voice returned through the feed.

"...if Arden reaches the vault, the archive becomes public... yes, I understand the risk... no, Anik has not issued a direct order..."

Senn cut the audio.

The room went quiet.

Quell stared at Wren. "They have an archive in the vault."

"The command vault contains operational keys."

"That was not what he said."

"You heard a fragment."

"You heard enough."

Rook's voice came over the repeater. "I am at the perimeter. Two security craft are moving toward me."

Lio pulled the outer-ring feed onto the display. The maintenance tug hovered above the command vault. Two fleet security craft approached from opposite vectors, their transponders broadcasting the yard commander's authority.

"Can you hold position?" Lio asked.

"I can hold position. They may not let me."

"Do not fire."

"I was not going to."

"You were thinking about it."

"That is different."

"Not today."

Rook did not answer.

The security craft issued a direct command.

**MAINTENANCE TUG: DISARM AND RETURN TO DOCK.**

Rook replied through the public channel.

"Maintenance Tug Fourteen acknowledges. We are operating under civilian rescue support and will maintain a visible perimeter while authority is reviewed."

The message went out under Rook's name.

The private system marked it as a second violation.

**SATO, R. — UNAUTHORIZED PUBLIC DISCLOSURE.**

Rook laughed once. "They are keeping score."

"They want a record of disobedience," Quell said.

"Then give them one they can read."

The first security craft moved closer.

Rook's craft rotated, keeping its repeater pointed toward the outer ring. The second security craft cut across its path.

"They are forcing me off the lane," Rook said.

"Hold."

"If I hold, they will collide."

"Yield."

"Then the perimeter opens."

Lio saw the geometry. The craft had placed Rook in a choice between collision and surrendering the vault approach. The move was not a mistake. The fleet security pilots knew exactly how to make a lawful command create the appearance of reckless resistance.

"Rook, take the lower vector."

"That puts me below the repair ring."

"It keeps you visible and clears their lane."

"It also puts me in the debris wake from the tug collision."

"Can you manage it?"

"Yes."

"Then do it."

Rook dropped the tug beneath the repair ring. The security craft crossed above him and continued toward the vault.

The move cost him position. It preserved his life and the public record of his compliance.

Quell watched the channel. "You are learning."

Lio did not look at her. "What?"

"The difference between acting and being seen to act."

"That is not a compliment."

"No."

At the command vault, the two inspectors reached the outer door.

The contractor stepped between them and the panel.

"The vault is under fleet security control," he said through the public channel.

Inspector Vennett replied, "You have not identified your authority."

"I am acting under a valid security directive."

"Issued by whom?"

The contractor did not answer.

The private channel opened in Lio's ear.

**SECURE THE VAULT.**

No name. No rank.

He closed the channel.

The command vault contained the evidence archive. The contractor was blocking the inspectors. The fleet security craft were moving into position. If Lio waited, the hidden system would decide the matter by physical control.

"I am sending the inspectors the access path," Senn said.

"Can you?"

"The public warrant has a valid court signature. It is the succession authority that is incomplete. If I route the warrant through the civilian maintenance layer, the vault may recognize the inspectors as evidence custodians."

"May?"

"The system is not designed for this combination."

"Do it."

Quell turned toward her. "Senn, that will place you inside the custody chain."

"It already has my name."

Senn routed the warrant.

The vault panel changed from red to amber.

**EVIDENCE CUSTODIAN REQUEST: REVIEW.**

The contractor stepped back.

Inspector Vennett placed her hand on the panel.

The fleet security craft closed around the vault.

The panel accepted the warrant.

The door opened three centimeters, then stopped.

The contractor reached for his private tool.

Lio opened the external security feed.

"Inspector Vennett, stay where you are."

"Who is speaking?"

"Cadet Commander Lio Arden. The door is being contested by an unverified security group."

"Can you hold them?"

"I can keep the system from accepting their access."

"That was not my question."

Lio looked at the two security craft, Rook's maintenance tug below the repair ring, Quell handling civilian traffic, and Senn's name entering another unauthorized record.

"No," he said. "I cannot hold them. I can make what they do visible."

Inspector Vennett was silent for half a second.

"Then make it visible."

Lio opened the yard emergency broadcast.

The camera feeds from the vault perimeter appeared across every maintenance panel, cockpit display, and public civilian receiver still connected to local power.

The contractor froze beside the door.

The two security craft stopped moving.

The entire yard could see the unverified security group, the valid civilian warrant, the command conflict, and the cadets operating the system.

The public channel filled with voices.

**WHO AUTHORIZED THE SECURITY DIRECTIVE?**

**WHY ARE CADETS ON THE YARD NETWORK?**

**IS THE COMMANDER IN CUSTODY?**

The contractor turned toward the nearest camera.

"This broadcast is an unauthorized interference with a military security operation," he said.

Inspector Vennett answered, "State your authority."

"The yard commander's authority is established."

"State your own."

The contractor hesitated.

That hesitation traveled through every receiver.

Lio felt the yard shift. The people watching did not need to know the entire conspiracy. They needed to see that the person demanding control could not name who had given him the right to demand it.

The private channel opened again.

**ARDEN, L. — CEASE BROADCAST.**

This time, the command carried an authentication mark.

Lio looked at it.

The mark belonged to Anik.

Quell saw his face. "What is it?"

"A direct order."

"From whom?"

He showed her the mark.

She read it.

"That is not a public authority."

"No."

"Then do not obey it."

Lio looked at Wren.

"Is Anik authorized to command this response?"

Wren did not answer immediately.

The silence was the closest thing to a confession she had given them.

"Wren."

"He has operational authority over the continuity channel."

"That is not what I asked."

"It is the authority he has."

"Who granted it?"

"The people who built the channel."

"Who?"

Wren's eyes moved to the public feed, where the contractor stood without a name and the inspectors waited with a warrant the fleet command could not erase.

"No one who will answer you here."

Lio kept the broadcast open.

Anik's order remained in the corner of the display.

**CEASE BROADCAST.**

The yard's civilian witnesses could see it now. The hidden command had exposed itself as another unverified instruction.

Rook's voice came through the repeater.

"The security craft are pulling back. They cannot fire with the public feed open."

Senn said, "The contractor is attempting to wipe the vault access logs."

"Can you stop him?"

"Yes, but I will need to lock the archive under my credentials."

Quell looked at Lio. "If she does, they can call it seizure."

"If she does not, they can erase it."

"There is no clean choice."

"I know."

Senn waited.

Lio looked at the public feed, the direct order from Anik, and the contractor's hand hovering over the vault controls.

"Lock the archive," he said.

Senn entered the command.

The vault sealed around the evidence.

The contractor's tool went dark.

A final message appeared on the public feed.

**ARCHIVE PRESERVED UNDER CADET SYSTEMS CUSTODY.**

The sentence was immediately followed by a private alert.

**VALE, S. — UNAUTHORIZED SEIZURE OF COMMAND EVIDENCE.**

Senn exhaled.

"They have another one."

"Let them keep writing."

The yard's power grid stabilized enough to restore the outer transfer ring. Civilian traffic began moving under Quell's manual directions. Rook returned to the maintenance tug lane. The inspectors entered the vault perimeter and secured the door.

The contractor and the fleet security officers stood under public observation without a detention order anyone could authenticate.

The crisis had not ended.

It had merely lost the ability to pretend that its authority was singular.

Wren looked at Lio across the red-lit operations floor.

"You have made the yard impossible to control."

"No," Lio said. "I have made the control visible."

"That is not the same as safety."

"Neither was secrecy."

The private channel went silent.

Then the academy network reconnected.

A delayed command appeared in the upper corner of the display.

**UNAUTHORIZED ACADEMY PERSONNEL DETECTED.**

Below it, the academy had added a second line.

**CADET COMMANDER ARDEN: COMMAND ACCESS REVOKED PENDING REVIEW.**

Quell read the message.

Rook came over the repeater. "They revoked your access?"

"Yes."

Senn checked their own console. "Mine too."

Quell looked at her panel. "Mine as well."

Rook's silence lasted longer.

"They have not revoked mine," he said.

Lio saw the separation immediately.

Rook's channel still carried the maintenance tug. His flight access remained active. The system was not punishing all of them. It was isolating the one person who had refused the direct order and making the others choose whether to remain operational.

A new message appeared on Rook's display.

**SATO, R. — CONTINUITY COMMAND RECOGNIZED.**

Rook read it aloud.

Nobody spoke.

The private channel opened in his ear only.

**SATO, R. SECURE THE COMMAND VAULT.**

Rook's voice came through the public repeater.

"They want me to take the vault."

Lio looked at him on the small craft feed.

"Do not."

"I know."

"Rook."

"I said I know."

His maintenance tug remained above the repair ring, visible to every camera.

The command vault was secured by civilian inspectors. The public channel was recording. The yard had enough power to survive the next hour.

For the first time since the crisis began, there was no immediate life at risk.

That was when the real choice arrived.

Rook opened the public channel.

"This is Pilot Rook Sato," he said. "I have received a private command to secure the vault. The command has no named public authority. I will not execute it."

The private channel marked another violation.

The yard's receivers carried the message outward.

Lio closed his eyes for one second.

When he opened them, the operations floor had changed again. The hidden command was still there. The crisis was still active. The fleet and civilian authorities still disagreed.

But the cohort no longer stood on one side of the command chain.

They were standing in public.
