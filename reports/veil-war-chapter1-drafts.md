# The Veil War: Chapter 1 Drafts By Option

## Option 1: The Refugee Fleet War

### Chapter 1 - Neutral Bell

The neutral bell rang at 0300 Halcyon time, which meant someone was about to lie.

Commander Mara Venn woke before the second tone. She had trained herself to do that after Pelagos, after the evacuation lights had turned every corridor red and the transport captains had started speaking in numbers instead of names. Sleep was a country she visited under guard. Alarms had visas there.

The bell sounded again through the walls of her cabin: three low notes, old bronze sampled from some lost planetary monastery and played through Halcyon Gate's public address system whenever a ship approached under claim of asylum.

One note for approach. One for witness. One for judgment.

Mara sat up in the dark. Beyond the narrow viewport, the Veil filled half the visible sky: a black storm of unlit dust, failed drives, and broken physics. Halcyon Gate drifted along its edge with a thousand wounded ships chained to its repair arms, valuable not because it guarded a road, but because it was the last place for three sectors where a fleet could be mended without asking an empire's permission.

Her wrist slate had already populated with reports.

UNSCHEDULED ARRIVAL.

SEVEN CIVILIAN HULLS.

IFF CORRUPTED OR REMOVED.

PURSUIT SIGNATURES DETECTED.

POTENTIAL CONCORDANCE TRACKING WAKE.

"Lights," she said.

The cabin obeyed. So did the mirror above the washstand, clearing from black to reflective silver. A woman stared back with close-cropped dark hair, one scar cutting through her left eyebrow, and the drawn face of someone who had not been surprised in years and did not consider that a virtue.

Her comm chimed before she reached for her jacket.

"Venn."

Tarek Sol's voice came through clean and soft. Intelligence officers always sounded as if they were standing in a quiet room, even when the hull was burning around them. "Seven ships out of Veil static. Civilian registry claims from Mirrorglass, though three hulls were built in Rhun yards and one is a converted ore dragger. Their transponders have been burned out."

"Survivors?"

"Thermal says crowded. Life support says overcrowded. Quill is already yelling at traffic control."

"About berths?"

"About theology."

That meant Father Ilyan was involved. Mara closed her eyes for half a second, long enough to see the shape of the problem. Asylum claim. Pursuit wake. Refugees. Priests. Smugglers. The Veil front. All before breakfast.

"Concordance ships?" she asked.

"None inside the perimeter."

"But?"

"But one stopped exactly where a legal officer would stop if they wanted us to make the first mistake."

Mara clipped on her sidearm and left it unopened. She had not fired it since taking command of Halcyon. She intended to keep that record until someone made intention a luxury.

Command Ring was already awake. The central display showed the seven refugee ships as pale shapes limping out of Veil static. They looked less like a convoy than debris that had learned formation from panic. One vented atmosphere in rhythmic white pulses. Another had lashed agricultural tanks along its spine, each tank packed with human life-signs.

Dockmaster Sera Quill stood in the command pit with one boot on a console base, copper coat thrown over a sleep shirt, hair pinned up with a stylus and what looked like a magnetic fuse.

"I can give you Dock Twelve if Engineering stops pretending those stress fractures are decorative," Quill said. "Dock Nine if Medical agrees not to scream about quarantine. Dock Four if the commander no longer values oxygen on this side of the city."

"I value oxygen," Mara said.

Quill turned. "Good. How do you feel about miracles?"

"I ration them."

"Then start with eight thousand people and three thousand emergency bunks."

The command pit went thin and quiet.

Mara looked at the ships again. Eight thousand was not a number. Not really. Numbers did not have toddlers held up to pressure-hatch cameras by mothers begging command to open one more lock.

"Put their lead vessel through," she said.

Static climbed the air before resolving into a woman in a cracked flight helmet. Blood floated in red beads along her cheek where low gravity refused to let it fall.

"Halcyon Gate," the woman said. "This is Kind Mercy out of Mirrorglass. We invoke neutral asylum under the Compact, clause seven, witness articles of the old armistice."

Quill muttered, "At least they read the sign."

Mara ignored her. "State the nature of your emergency."

The pilot laughed once, stripped of humor. "Occupation."

"Mirrorglass is under Concordance protectorate negotiation, not occupation."

"That is what they call it before the singing starts."

Father Ilyan entered without authorization, gray robe belted wrong, luminous tattoo-script moving faintly under the skin of his cheek. He heard the pilot's last words and went still.

"Who taught you that phrase?" he asked.

The pilot looked at him, and fear broke through her exhaustion. "A child."

The display expanded. Beyond the refugees, hanging in the wash of Veil static, a single marker appeared and matched Halcyon's drift at the edge of the declared neutral perimeter.

Concordance.

The incoming transmission carried no static.

Ambassador Veyr appeared beneath a white arch, hairless, elegant, wearing the pale formal coat of civil authority. No soldiers appeared behind them. No threat needed the courtesy of being visible.

"Commander Venn," Veyr said. "My apologies for the hour."

"Ambassador."

"A group of distressed Mirrorglass citizens has fled a lawful transition zone with stolen vessels, stolen supplies, and one protected minor whose guardianship falls under Concordance treaty supervision. We request permission to cross the perimeter, render aid, and retrieve our ward."

On the refugee channel, the pilot made a small broken sound.

Mara looked at the seven ships. At the venting hull. At the thermal bloom of eight thousand people waiting to learn whether neutrality meant anything when tested by empire.

"Name the minor," she said.

Veyr's expression did not change. "Nima Vale."

Father Ilyan gripped the rail.

"Under Compact law," Veyr said gently, "asylum cannot be used to shield abduction, insurgency, or unlawful retention of protected persons. We are prepared to offer guarantees for every other passenger. Food. Medicine. Safe resettlement. No punitive measures."

"In exchange for the child," Mara said.

"In exchange for the restoration of a legal ward."

The words were polished smooth enough to show no blood.

Mara opened a private line to Tarek. "Can we hold them out?"

"Militarily? Briefly. Legally? Depends which judge is scared. Politically? No."

"Useful as always."

"You asked three questions."

She cut the channel.

Veyr waited. They had patience as a weapon. Return one child. Save eight thousand. Preserve neutrality. Keep Halcyon from becoming the match head struck against the frontier.

It was a good offer.

That was what made it obscene.

"Ambassador Veyr," Mara said, "Halcyon Gate recognizes the asylum claim."

"For the convoy?"

"For all souls aboard the convoy."

"Commander, I urge precision."

"So do I."

For the first time, regret touched the ambassador's face. "If you admit the child, you transform a humanitarian matter into a treaty breach."

"No," Mara said. "You did that when you chased refugees to my door and asked me to call one of them property."

Veyr studied her. "You have a reputation for regretting rescue mathematics."

Pelagos entered the room.

Mara felt the old wound open and refused to look down.

"Then you should have known better than to bring me arithmetic," she said.

She cut the channel.

For one second, Command Ring remained perfectly silent.

Then every alarm in Halcyon began to speak at once.

## Option 2: The Carrier Alliance Campaign

### Chapter 1 - Neutral Bell

By the time the neutral bell rang, Halcyon Gate was already losing air.

Not much. Not enough to panic anyone who had not spent their life learning how machines lied before they failed. Just a tenth-point atmospheric drift along Dock Twelve, then another in the surgical annex, then a pressure burp in the old refinery spine that made every loose panel in Command Ring tremble.

Sera Quill noticed before the alarms did.

"We are not taking another hull," she said.

Commander Mara Venn looked up from the main display. Seven refugee ships hung in the Veil static, burned and overcrowded. Their lead vessel, Kind Mercy, had split a seam across her port side. Dock crews in pressure suits clung to her like insects on a dying animal, laying sealant foam over metal too tired to hold shape.

"We are taking all seven," Mara said.

"Then we are giving them our air and hoping they return it before we notice."

"Do you have a better plan?"

Quill's smile was small and unpleasant. "Yes. Refuse miracles before they become policy."

The bell rang again: approach, witness, judgment.

Mara let the sound pass through the command pit. Everyone was listening. Everyone knew what the bell meant. Halcyon had been built from six drydocks, a hospital tender, and a captured refinery spine; law was one of the few things holding the welds together.

"Kind Mercy has invoked asylum," Mara said. "We honor the claim."

The refugee pilot appeared on the display, helmet cracked, blood floating along her cheek in low gravity. "Commander, we have children in coolant lockers because the decks are too hot. We can't run and we can't go back."

"You are cleared for emergency docking," Mara said. "Follow Quill's vectors exactly."

Quill muttered, "No pressure."

"There will be pressure," Mara said. "That is why we have doors."

Tarek Sol's intelligence station chimed. He went very still.

Mara disliked stillness in intelligence officers. It usually meant they had found something too sharp to pick up with bare hands.

"Report."

"New contacts," Tarek said. "Coming out of Veil static. Not Concordance."

The main display expanded. At first the contacts looked like debris: fragments tumbling on ugly vectors, too hot in the wrong places. Then Halcyon's systems found engines under the damage and drew a hull around the largest shape.

The command pit changed temperature.

Armored prow. Broken flight deck. Heat-scored launch spines. Dark red ceramic plating that made the sensors stutter.

Quill exhaled through her teeth. "Kharu."

Someone in the back of the pit swore. Someone else crossed themselves in a faith Mara did not know.

The Kharu had been humanity's enemy before the Concordance made old hatred feel like an expensive hobby. They boarded disabled ships with blade teams, transmitted poetry before attack runs, and painted kill marks in family blood pigment. Human children learned their bronze masks before they learned evacuation routes.

Now a Kharu carrier was limping toward Halcyon with half its flight deck gone.

"Weapons?" Mara asked.

"Hot but not locked," Tarek said. "Reactor unstable. Two squadrons operational at most."

"Only two," Quill said. "How comforting."

The carrier opened a channel without permission.

A Kharu officer filled the display, larger than human by half, skin deep umber ridged along brow and jaw. Four ritual scars crossed his throat. One was fresh enough to shine.

"Halcyon Gate," he said, turning the name into a challenge. "I am Wing-Captain Rauk var Kesh of the Blood-of-Ash. I claim repair-right under the frontier armistice."

Quill leaned toward Mara. "Technically valid."

"Technically obscene," Tarek said.

Rauk bared his teeth. "Your whisper-man objects."

"My whisper-man objects professionally," Mara said. "State your damage."

"We fought three Concordance cutters beyond the Veil front. We killed one. We wounded another. The third ran behind its law-shields." His nostrils flared. "Our launch deck is torn. Our dead are impatient. We require metal, fuel, and a place to put our wounded where your people will not murder them in their beds."

The refugee pilot was still on the secondary display. She stared at Rauk with a hatred too exhausted to stand upright.

"They raided Mirrorglass farms," she said.

Rauk did not look at her. "And Mirrorglass sold targeting maps to Rhun privateers. Shall we recite the whole century while your children suffocate?"

Quill whispered, "This is going well."

Mara studied the displays. Refugees on one side. Kharu on the other. Concordance somewhere beyond the static, patient enough to let hatred do its work.

"Wing-Captain," she said, "if I grant repair-right, you will power down spinal weapons, surrender flight control codes inside our perimeter, and confine armed personnel to assigned decks."

"You ask me to enter your den toothless."

"I ask you to enter it housebroken."

Quill made a choking sound that was almost laughter.

For a heartbeat, Mara thought Rauk might refuse. Instead he struck his fist against his chest plate hard enough for the mic to catch the impact.

"Terms accepted. Break them and I will write your name in the old meter."

"Break them and I will vent your launch deck."

This time the teeth were definitely a smile.

The channel closed.

The command pit exploded into overlapping objections.

Mara raised one hand. The objections died badly but died.

"Dock Twelve takes Kind Mercy. Dock Nine takes civilian overflow. Outer pylon three takes Blood-of-Ash. No Kharu personnel past Blue Line without escort. No refugee mobs within sightline of outer pylon three. Quill, make the impossible look scheduled."

"I charge extra for blasphemy."

"Approved."

Tarek stepped closer. "You just built a defense screen and a riot."

"Both can be useful if pointed correctly."

"That was almost a joke."

"Log it for morale."

The neutral bell rang a third time, though no new ship had approached.

Across the display, Concordance pursuit signatures bloomed at the edge of the Veil static, pale and precise as knives laid on a table.

Mara looked from the refugees to the Kharu carrier.

The war had not arrived as an enemy fleet.

It had arrived as a seating arrangement.

## Option 3: The Occupation Without Invasion

### Chapter 1 - Asylum Claim

Ambassador Veyr did not threaten Halcyon Gate.

That was the first thing Mara Venn distrusted.

The refugee convoy had arrived at 0300 Halcyon time, seven civilian hulls limping out of Veil static with burned transponders, damaged seals, and too many heat signatures packed into spaces meant for cargo. Their lead vessel invoked neutral asylum under the Compact. Mara accepted because the alternative was sending eight thousand people back into the hands of an empire polite enough to call occupation transition.

Then Veyr appeared on the screen in flawless resolution and asked for one child.

Nima Vale.

The name had moved through Command Ring like a dropped blade. Father Ilyan had gone pale. Tarek Sol had stopped typing. Sera Quill had whispered something profane and sincere.

Mara refused.

That should have been the moment the Concordance vessel crossed the perimeter or lit weapons or launched drones through the wounded convoy. Instead Veyr inclined their head with faint regret.

"Then we request observer status," they said.

Mara stared at the display. "No."

"Commander, the Concordance recognizes Halcyon's neutral authority. We ask only to document the disposition of Mirrorglass civilians under your temporary care, confirm the safety of our legal ward, and ensure no unlawful combatants use asylum to mask insurgent activity."

"You asked for a child. I refused. Now you want a chair."

"I want a record."

Veyr's voice remained mild. Mildness was what they used instead of armor.

The command pit watched Mara watch the ambassador.

Tarek opened a private channel. "Legally ugly."

"For them?"

"For us. Observer status is allowed after disputed asylum claims if the requesting power agrees not to exercise police authority."

"And if I reject it?"

"You look like you are hiding insurgents."

Quill joined the private line without invitation. "We are hiding insurgents?"

"Quill."

"Sorry. We are providing temporary religious-adjacent hospitality to politically inconvenient minors."

Mara muted them both.

On the secondary display, the refugee pilot from Kind Mercy waited in her cracked helmet, blood drying along her cheek. Behind her, people stood packed shoulder to shoulder in a cargo bay. A child slept upright because there was no room to fall.

Mara had already accepted the moral burden. Now Veyr was offering the legal one.

"Observer status does not permit armed personnel inside Halcyon," Mara said.

"Agreed."

"No independent movement."

"Agreed."

"No contact with Nima Vale."

For the first time, Veyr paused.

There it was. The seam in the fabric.

"We request visual confirmation of her welfare."

"Denied."

"That will create concern."

"Let it."

Veyr inclined their head again. "Then we accept limited observer status under protest."

"You protest efficiently."

"We have had practice being misunderstood."

The channel closed.

Quill turned slowly. "Did we just invite an empire into our waiting room?"

"We invited a clerk," Mara said.

Tarek looked at the empty display. "That was not a clerk."

By 0600, the first Concordance observer shuttle crossed the perimeter. It was white, unarmed, and graceful, shaped like a seed pod from a world that had never known artillery. Halcyon's defense systems tracked it anyway. The shuttle docked under full public feed because Mara refused to let secrecy do Veyr's work for them.

Three observers emerged.

No soldiers. No weapons. No raised voices.

They brought medical kits.

That was the second thing Mara distrusted.

The kits were efficient, sealed, sterile, and desperately needed. Doctor Pell stared at the crates in Dock Twelve as if hate alone might manufacture antibiotics.

"Commander," Pell said over the medical net, "I can refuse these on principle and lose thirty people before noon, or accept them and feel dirty while they live."

Mara closed her eyes. This was how occupation began when it had read the room.

"Use them," she said. "Mark every dose. Track every patient."

"And if there is nothing wrong with the medicine?"

"Then we thank them."

Pell laughed once. "That hurt you."

"Log the pain."

By 0900, Concordance aid offers had reached individual ships through channels Halcyon had not authorized. Food allotments. Debt forgiveness. Safe conduct. Medical care. Resettlement guarantees.

All voluntary.

All legal.

All addressed to captains who had not slept, crews who had not eaten, parents holding feverish children in corridors that smelled of coolant and fear.

The first captain requested detachment at 0947.

Mara read the petition twice. Free-crew vessel Larkspur. Forty-seven souls. No direct tie to Nima. No criminal charge. No tactical value. They wanted to leave Halcyon and accept Concordance review.

Quill stood across from her in Command Ring, arms folded. "If you deny it, you are holding them."

"I know."

"If you allow it, more will go."

"I know that too."

Tarek said nothing, which meant he agreed with both of them and hated the geometry.

On the main display, Veyr's observer shuttle remained docked, white and patient.

No invasion came.

No guns.

No threat.

Just offers, contracts, medicine, and the slow discovery that Halcyon's people could be separated one exhausted signature at a time.

Mara approved Larkspur's detachment.

As the little ship peeled away from Halcyon's repair arm and turned toward the Concordance marker, the neutral bell rang once.

Not approach. Not witness. Not judgment.

Departure.

The sound was worse.

## Option 4: The Witness-Child Betrayal

### Chapter 1 - The Forbidden Child

The child did not look forbidden.

That was the first problem.

Nima Vale sat on the floor of the chapel enclave with a silver emergency blanket around her shoulders, bare feet tucked beneath her, and a cup of broth cooling untouched between both hands. She looked nine or ten, though refugee children often carried age badly. Her hair had been cut short with a dull blade. A bruise shadowed one cheekbone. Someone had written a string of numbers along the inside of her left wrist in black marker, then tried to scrub them off.

Commander Mara Venn stood at the edge of the enclave and did not enter.

The Lumen chapel was not large. Halcyon had no room for large sacred spaces. It was a converted navigation blister with the old glass plated over from outside, leaving a curved wall where worshippers had etched names instead of stars. Thousands of them. Dead, missing, unborn, disputed. Father Ilyan called it the Ledger Wall. Quill called it a fire hazard with incense.

Nima stared at the wall as if some of the names were staring back.

"She hasn't spoken since Dock Twelve," Sister-Ledger Elian said.

Elian was older than Mara by twenty years and looked older than that by choice. Her gray robe had been patched at the elbows. Her hands were stained with ink from writing names onto cloth strips for refugees who had arrived without documents.

"She spoke on the ship," Mara said.

"Children often spend words in order to reach safety, then discover they borrowed them at interest."

Mara did not have the patience for priestly grammar. "The Concordance says she is a legal ward."

Ilyan, standing beside the Ledger Wall, gave a soft sound. "Of course they do."

"Meaning?"

"Meaning they have many words for ownership."

Nima's hands tightened around the cup.

Mara saw it. So did Elian.

Ilyan went very still. "Nima."

The child did not look away from the names.

"They asked for me," she said.

Her voice was small, roughened by dehydration and disuse. Not mystical. Not prophetic. Just a child's voice, which made the command ring recording in Mara's mind sound uglier.

"Yes," Mara said.

"Did you give me back?"

"No."

Nima nodded once, as if confirming a detail in a lesson. "They said people like you always do after counting."

Pelagos opened beneath Mara's ribs.

She kept her voice even. "People like me?"

"Command people."

Elian closed her eyes.

Mara stepped into the chapel. The air smelled of broth, metal, and the citrus oil the Lumen used to clean surfaces before writing names. She crouched far enough from Nima that the child would not have to retreat to keep distance.

"I count," Mara said. "I did not give you back."

Nima looked at her then. Her eyes were dark, alert, and too practiced at measuring adult usefulness.

"Why?"

There were clean answers. Law. Asylum. The Compact. Personhood. Mara had spent a career learning how to make choices sound like documents.

"Because they called you a ward and meant property."

Nima considered that.

"They will come again."

"I know."

"They will ask nicer."

"I know that too."

The child lifted the cup and drank one small swallow. Elian's shoulders loosened by a fraction.

Mara's comm vibrated at her wrist. Tarek. She ignored it.

"Nima," Ilyan said gently, "the verse you sent through Kind Mercy. Who taught it to you?"

The cup trembled.

Mara glanced at him.

Ilyan's face had changed. Priests were dangerous when they stopped sounding comforting.

"What verse?" Mara asked.

Elian answered. "The convoy broadcast a Lumen funeral cant under its distress static. Not a common one. A proscribed one."

"Proscribed by whom?"

"By us."

Mara let that sit.

Nima stared into the broth. "I heard it in the white room."

"What white room?" Mara asked.

"The room where they made people agree."

No one moved.

Outside the plated glass, Halcyon groaned softly as another refugee ship locked into a repair arm. The whole fleet-city seemed to lean closer.

Nima began to hum.

It was barely sound at first. A child's thread of pitch, unsteady and thin. Then the Ledger Wall answered.

Not with music. With light.

Three names near the center of the wall brightened under their etched strokes. Then seven. Then twelve. Old ink lifted from metal like embers waking under ash.

Elian whispered a word Mara did not know.

Ilyan reached for the wall and stopped before touching it.

Mara stood slowly. "What is happening?"

Nima stopped humming.

The names went dark.

Her face had gone empty in the way exhausted children sometimes vanished while still sitting in front of you.

"I don't know," she said.

Mara believed her.

That was the second problem.

Her comm vibrated again. This time Tarek overrode privacy.

"Commander," he said, voice tight. "Veyr is transmitting a formal custody demand to every ship in the fleet. They included Nima's medical record."

Mara looked at the child.

"What medical record?"

Tarek hesitated.

"According to the Concordance," he said, "Nima Vale died six months ago."

The cup slipped from Nima's hands and spilled broth across the chapel floor.

No one moved to clean it.

## Option 5: The Fall Of Halcyon

### Chapter 1 - Fleet-City

Halcyon Gate had survived by refusing to be one thing.

It was six military drydocks welded to a hospital tender. It was a captured refinery spine wrapped in civilian markets. It was a court, a fuel depot, a cemetery, a machine shop, a chapel row, a smuggler harbor, and a command platform that every government claimed to respect because none of them could afford to replace it.

It was not a city, not legally.

It was not a fleet, not tactically.

It was not a nation, not yet.

Commander Mara Venn stood in the observation blister above Command Ring and watched maintenance lights crawl across the outer arms like fireflies trapped in a skeleton. Beyond them drifted the dependent ships: tankers, cutters, barges, family freighters, hospital pods, salvage rigs, two obsolete destroyers, and a children’s schoolship painted yellow by people who either had courage or had run out of regulation gray.

A thousand hulls chained to one another by docking collars, debt, fear, and need.

Halcyon.

It looked permanent from a distance. That was the lie people needed from cities.

Her comm chimed.

"Venn."

Sera Quill's voice came through, already irritated. "Before you ask, yes, I know Dock Twelve is over pressure tolerance. No, I did not authorize the extra fuel bladders. Yes, I know someone did, because fuel bladders do not migrate in herds."

"Good morning to you too."

"Morning is a planetary superstition."

Mara turned from the glass. "What happened?"

"Seven civilian hulls just stumbled out of Veil static. Burned transponders. Bad seals. Lots of warm bodies. One is venting. One is lying about its mass. All are about to become our problem because the neutral bell is stretching its throat."

As if summoned, the first tone rolled through Halcyon: low, bronze, and old.

Approach.

Mara closed her eyes.

The second tone followed.

Witness.

By the third, she was already moving.

Command Ring had the particular calm of professionals doing arithmetic with lives while pretending arithmetic was not a form of prayer. The main display showed the incoming ships in pale outline. They looked small against Halcyon's sprawl, but the life-sign estimates climbed with every sensor pass.

Tarek Sol stood at intelligence, hands moving lightly over his console. "Registry claims Mirrorglass. Pursuit signatures behind them, consistent with Concordance tracking wakes. No hostile ship inside our perimeter."

"Yet," Quill said.

Mara looked at the numbers.

Eight thousand refugees.

Halcyon could absorb three thousand without systemic damage. Four if she shut down two fabrication bays and converted the customs court into a ward. Five if she lied to Medical and prayed to engineering tolerances. Eight meant something broke.

Not immediately. Not theatrically. Cities rarely died in explosions. They died by becoming worse at everything until someone decided leaving was survival.

"Lead vessel is requesting channel," Tarek said.

"Put them through."

The pilot of Kind Mercy appeared in broken resolution, helmet cracked, blood floating in low gravity. Behind her, people stood packed into a cargo bay meant for grain.

"Halcyon Gate," she said. "We invoke neutral asylum under the Compact."

Quill muttered, "Of course you do."

Mara kept her eyes on the pilot. "State your emergency."

"Mirrorglass accepted protectorate negotiation. The Concordance arrived with food, judges, teachers, medical ships." Her mouth twisted. "Then the singing started."

Father Ilyan entered Command Ring at the wrong moment, which meant he had probably known before anyone summoned him. His robe was belted badly. Sacred script glowed under the skin of his cheek.

"What singing?" Mara asked.

The pilot looked at Ilyan. "Ask your priest."

Ilyan did not look away from the screen. "How many children?"

"Alive?"

No one in Command Ring breathed correctly after that.

Mara made the decision because not making it would also be a decision, and she had learned on Pelagos that delay liked to dress itself as prudence.

"All seven ships are cleared for emergency docking," she said.

Quill turned. "Mara."

The use of her first name inside Command Ring cut through the room.

"Dock Twelve, Nine, and Four," Mara said. "Convert the customs court. Wake Pell. Ration nonessential water. Tell every free crew captain chained to our hull that I am invoking burden clause."

Quill's face hardened. "They will say you are taking their reserves."

"I am."

"They will detach."

"Not before the locks cycle."

Tarek's console chimed. "Concordance marker at perimeter edge."

There it was. The other shoe, placed gently rather than dropped.

Ambassador Veyr appeared on the main display in flawless resolution. White coat. Calm eyes. No visible soldiers. No visible weapon. Just civilization as a threat.

"Commander Venn," Veyr said. "My apologies. We seem to have misplaced several distressed citizens."

Mara heard Quill swear softly.

The refugee pilot closed her eyes.

Veyr continued. "We request permission to render aid and recover one protected minor unlawfully removed from our care."

"Name the minor," Mara said.

"Nima Vale."

Ilyan gripped the railing.

Mara saw it. So did Veyr.

Outside the observation blister, Halcyon's repair lights crawled over a thousand dependent hulls. A city that was not a city. A nation that was not a nation. A thing too useful to be free and too crowded to be brave without cost.

"Request denied," Mara said.

Veyr's expression did not change. "Then you accept responsibility for everyone aboard those ships."

Mara looked at the life-sign numbers. Eight thousand added to a machine already carrying too much.

"Yes," she said.

The word was small.

Halcyon groaned around it.

For the first time since Mara took command, the city sounded old.
