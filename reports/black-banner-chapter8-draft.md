# Black Banner Academy: Chapter 8 Draft

## Continuity Scenario

The footsteps stopped outside the registrar annex.

Lio kept one hand on the folder and the other near the black pin.

The office had no second exit. The windows were narrow, reinforced, and six floors above the academy's lower transit spine. The door stood open because whoever had invited him wanted him to see the corridor before they entered it.

"Resolve the channel," the voice said again.

Lio recognized it now.

Commander Wren.

She stood in the corridor alone. The lights behind her had been dimmed, leaving her face divided between the ordinary academy and the darker passage toward the service lift.

"Where are the others?" Lio asked.

"Not here."

"Who prepared this file?"

"You have five seconds to decide whether you are coming with me."

"That is not an answer."

"Four."

Lio looked at the folder.

He wanted to take it. The evidence inside could prove the suspension had been planned, that Iven's investigation was not a surprise, that Black Banner had anticipated the channel before Iven opened it. He could hide the folder under his jacket and walk toward Wren as if obedience were a form of concealment.

He could also leave it where it was and make the file a marker for whoever came after him.

"Three."

Lio closed the folder.

He did not touch it again.

"Two."

He walked into the corridor.

Wren did not look at the folder. That was the first thing that told him she had known what the choice would be.

"Was that a test?" he asked.

"Everything is a test when you are being watched."

"You sent the message."

"No."

"Anik did?"

"No."

"Then who?"

Wren started toward the service lift. "You are assuming there is only one channel because that is what they want you to assume."

The service lift opened before she touched the call panel.

Inside, Quell stood alone.

She had removed her formal jacket and carried it over one arm. The black pin was gone from her uniform.

"You too?" Lio said.

"I was asked to attend a legal review."

"Where is the review?"

"The basement."

Wren entered the lift. Lio followed. Quell remained near the rear wall, eyes on the panel.

The doors closed.

For several seconds, the lift did not move.

Then it descended past the academy levels without displaying their numbers.

"Senn copied the risk directory," Quell said. "Rook is with them."

"Where?"

"I don't know."

"You don't know, or you are not allowed to say?"

Quell looked at him. "I don't know."

The lift passed Sublevel Eight.

Wren stood with her hands behind her back. The campaign ribbon at her throat reflected a narrow line of light.

"The folder in the annex," Lio said. "It named Iven before he was involved."

Wren's jaw tightened.

"That means the program knew he would investigate."

"It means the program knows what kinds of people investigate."

"It knew the clerk would be suspended."

"The academy suspended the clerk."

"Because Black Banner told it to?"

Wren looked at the lift doors. "You are asking questions as though a clean answer would make the structure cleaner."

"I am asking who authorized the file."

"The file was authorized by people who believe authorization is a clerical problem."

The lift stopped.

The doors opened on the operations floor.

The tables had been cleared. No coffee. No crew stations. No familiar interface. In the center of the room stood a circular platform surrounded by four consoles, each separated by a low transparent wall.

Senn was at one console.

Rook stood at another, already angry.

Anik waited beside the plot table.

The folder from the registrar annex appeared on the central display.

Lio stopped walking.

"You left it behind," Anik said.

"I know."

"Good."

Quell crossed the room to her console. "What is this?"

"A continuity scenario."

"You already used that designation."

"We used it yesterday. Today you will be asked to understand it."

Anik touched the plot table.

A system map rose above the platform.

The image showed an orbital yard, three civilian transfer rings, two fleet repair docks, and a chain of relay stations running through a dark section of space. A ministry shuttle blinked at the center of the map.

The scenario had a title.

**SUCCESSION EVENT: COMMAND AUTHORITY DISPUTED.**

Beneath it, another line appeared.

**YARD CONTINUITY AT RISK.**

Rook looked at the map. "Is this based on a real event?"

"No," Anik said.

Senn's hand moved across their console. "The yard identifiers are real."

"The identifiers are available in public fleet records."

"The repair dock schedule is not."

Anik's gaze shifted to her. "Then the scenario will be useful."

Quell folded her arms. "What is the legal authority?"

"You are the legal authority."

"That is a role, not an answer."

"The civilian ministerial shuttle has been destroyed. The fleet commander at the yard claims emergency succession authority. Civilian inspectors have arrived with a warrant. An unknown sabotage threat is moving through the repair network. You have twenty minutes to prevent the yard from losing power."

"Who authorized the emergency succession?"

"The fleet commander."

"Who authorized the warrant?"

"The civilian court."

"Which one is valid?"

"You will determine that."

Quell looked at Lio. "This is not a command exercise. It is a jurisdiction exercise."

"Most emergencies are both," Wren said.

Anik turned to the cadets.

"Cadet Arden holds command. Cadet Quell holds civilian authority. Cadet Sato controls yard defense and rescue craft. Cadet Vale controls systems, traffic, and evidence capture."

Senn glanced at the empty space beside their console. "Evidence capture?"

"You will record the decisions made and the authority invoked for each."

"Who gets the record?"

"The record will be preserved."

"That is not what I asked."

Anik smiled slightly. "Begin."

The timer started.

The yard appeared in full detail. Docked repair vessels. Civilian workers moving through transfer rings. A ministry shuttle spinning apart near the central relay. The fleet commander's emergency claim appeared on one channel, followed by the civilian warrant on another.

The two orders contradicted each other.

**FLEET EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: SECURE YARD COMMAND VAULT.**

**CIVILIAN INSPECTION WARRANT: PRESERVE EVIDENCE AND DETAIN NO PERSON WITHOUT REVIEW.**

Lio opened the systems layer.

"Senn, locate the power fault."

"Outer relay three. It is cycling under manual override."

"Rook, get rescue craft to the transfer rings."

"Which rings?"

"All three."

"I have two craft."

"Prioritize the central ring."

Quell turned toward him. "The civilian warrant requires preservation of the outer evidence channel."

"People are losing pressure in the central ring."

"The evidence may establish whether the attack was internal."

"Then get Senn to preserve it while Rook rescues the workers."

"That is not a lawful allocation of command authority."

"It is an allocation of tasks."

"No. It is a command decision made before the authority question is resolved."

The yard lights flickered.

A new warning appeared.

**REPAIR DOCK FOUR: ATMOSPHERIC LOSS.**

Rook launched one rescue craft.

Quell stared at the display. "You gave an order."

"Yes."

"Under whose authority?"

Lio watched the pressure numbers fall.

"Mine until someone with a better one stops me."

Quell's expression went flat. "That is not how authority works."

"It is how pressure works."

The words had barely left him when a third communication channel opened.

**CIVILIAN INSPECTOR GROUP REQUESTS ACCESS TO COMMAND VAULT.**

The fleet commander's channel responded immediately.

**INSPECTORS COMPROMISED. DETAIN ON CONTACT.**

The detention protocol appeared beneath the order.

It was not a simulation prompt. It was a real executable procedure with authorization fields, custody categories, and transfer destinations.

Senn stared at it.

"This is live."

"The scenario is live enough to be useful," Wren said.

"That is not what I said. The detention protocol can execute against an actual facility."

Quell stepped toward the plot table. "Lio, do not authorize it."

Rook's rescue craft entered Dock Four. The atmospheric loss accelerated.

Lio opened the command vault map. The vault contained yard control keys, emergency power routing, and the evidence archive the civilian inspectors had requested.

If the inspectors reached it first, they could lock the fleet commander out. If the fleet commander detained them, the warrant would become meaningless. If Lio delayed, the yard's outer ring could lose pressure before Rook completed the rescue.

The scenario had put every reasonable answer on a clock.

"Senn, can you isolate the detention protocol?"

"Yes, but it will expose the command channel."

"Do it."

"That makes us visible to both sides."

"We are already visible."

Senn's fingers moved.

The detention protocol split into two layers: the order itself and the executable access path. She severed the path. The order remained on the display, red and undeniable, but no one could carry it out through the system.

The fleet commander's channel changed.

**UNAUTHORIZED INTERFERENCE DETECTED.**

Rook's voice came through the rescue channel. "I have thirty-two workers in Dock Four. The second craft is inbound."

"Keep the dock sealed until pressure stabilizes."

"That will trap the second craft outside."

"I know."

"Then I am asking whether you want them alive or orderly."

Lio looked at the command vault. The inspectors were thirty seconds away.

"Bring them through the maintenance lock."

"That route is not rated for a rescue craft."

"Use it anyway."

Quell turned sharply. "You cannot keep making illegal choices and call them necessary."

"Then give me a legal one."

She looked at the map.

The words did not come quickly. Lio saw her trying to find a position that would be both legitimate and useful, and the scenario gave her no time to complete the search.

"I can issue a civilian preservation order," she said. "It would prevent both sides from altering the evidence while the warrant is verified."

"Can it stop the fleet commander from taking the vault?"

"If the system recognizes civilian authority."

"Will it?"

"It should."

"Should is not enough."

"It is what lawful systems are built from."

Quell entered the order.

The system accepted it.

For one second, the two channels went quiet.

Then the fleet commander rejected the order.

**CIVILIAN AUTHORITY NOT RECOGNIZED DURING SUCCESSION EVENT.**

Quell's face tightened.

"The command network does not recognize me."

"It recognizes the commander."

"The commander claims emergency authority."

"And the inspectors have a warrant."

"Then we have three incompatible claims and no shared authority layer."

Senn looked at the evidence channel. "The warrant's authentication is incomplete."

"Incomplete how?" Lio asked.

"The court signature is valid, but the succession event has not been entered into the civilian registry. The warrant cannot attach to a command authority the civil system does not know exists."

Quell stared at the screen.

The *Asterion* lecture returned to Lio: speed was not authority. It was only one quality authority might require.

"Can you create the registry entry?" he asked.

Senn looked at him. "That would be falsifying a public record."

"Can you create a temporary status pending review?"

"That would still be an unauthorized entry."

"Can you make the system show the gap?"

She understood before he finished.

"I can force the civilian registry to return an authority conflict. It will pause the warrant and the succession claim while the conflict is reviewed."

"How long?"

"Long enough to save Dock Four. Not long enough to solve the yard."

"Do it."

Senn opened the registry interface.

The system presented a warning.

**UNAUTHORIZED ACCESS. RECORD WILL IDENTIFY OPERATOR.**

She looked at Lio.

"It will be my name."

"I know."

"You are asking me to commit a documented violation."

"I am asking you to make the system show that neither authority can currently be verified."

"That is a distinction the system may not respect."

"Do you?"

Senn's eyes moved to the timer.

Eleven minutes remained.

She entered the command.

The registry rejected the succession event, accepted the warrant for review, and opened a public conflict record.

The command vault locked.

The yard's defense systems paused.

Every channel displayed the same message.

**AUTHORITY CONFLICT: REVIEW REQUIRED.**

Rook's rescue craft cleared Dock Four through the maintenance lock, its hull scraping the guide rails. Thirty-two workers entered the transfer ring alive.

The scenario did not end.

The fleet commander's system began a manual power cut.

"They are going to drop the yard," Senn said.

"Can you stop it?" Lio asked.

"Not without overriding the command authority."

Quell looked at him. "Do not."

Lio watched the power graph fall. The yard's outer ring dimmed. Civilian traffic stalled. The repair vessels lost their guidance beacons.

Wren stepped closer to the platform.

"This is the point of the exercise."

Lio looked at her. "Which point?"

"The point where lawful delay becomes a form of harm."

"Who wrote that into the scenario?"

Wren said nothing.

"Who authorized the detention protocol?"

Still nothing.

"Who decided the civilian warrant should be incomplete?"

Anik stood beside the dark edge of the plot table.

"You are wasting time."

Lio turned toward him. "You want us to override the commander."

"I want the yard to remain operational."

"That is not an answer."

"It is the answer that matters."

The words were almost the same as Lio's from the previous day.

He felt the resemblance as a pressure against his ribs.

Rook's voice broke through. "The workers are clear. I can make another run, but the transfer ring is losing power."

Quell looked at the falling graph.

"Lio," she said, "if you override the commander, you are creating an authority the system will treat as valid because it works."

"If I do nothing, people die."

"Yes."

"Then what do you suggest?"

Quell looked at the public conflict record.

The record displayed every rejected claim, every failed authentication, and Senn's unauthorized registry entry. Anyone with access could see that the command structure had failed before the cadets entered it.

"Do not override the commander," she said. "Make the commander override the public record."

Lio understood.

"Senn, mirror the conflict record to the yard's emergency broadcast."

"That will expose every authority dispute."

"Yes."

"It will expose us."

"Yes."

Rook laughed through the rescue channel. "I am beginning to suspect that is the curriculum."

Senn mirrored the record.

The yard's public system came alive. The authority conflict, the detention order, the incomplete warrant, and the rejected civilian preservation order appeared across every station display.

The fleet commander attempted to close the broadcast.

The power cut stopped.

The scenario froze.

The timer halted at seven minutes, twelve seconds.

For a moment, nobody spoke.

Then the plot table displayed a final assessment.

**OUTCOME: YARD PRESERVED. AUTHORITY UNRESOLVED.**

Below it:

**CADET ARDEN: OPERATIONAL JUDGMENT ACCEPTED.**

**CADET QUELL: LEGAL RESISTANCE INCOMPLETE.**

**CADET SATO: RESPONSE TIME EXCELLENT.**

**CADET VALE: SYSTEM INTEGRITY BREACH CONFIRMED.**

Senn read their line twice.

"They are calling the evidence a breach."

"It was a breach," Anik said.

"It preserved the authority conflict."

"It also exposed restricted systems."

Quell looked at the frozen display. "Who won?"

Anik smiled without warmth.

"You are still asking the wrong question."

The displays went dark.

The room lights returned.

The platform was only a platform again. The consoles were only consoles. The central folder from the registrar annex had vanished from the display.

Lio looked at Wren.

"Was that a simulation?"

Wren met his eyes.

"It was an exercise."

"That is not what I asked."

"No. It is the answer you have."

The service lift opened.

No one had called it.

Senn removed the memory wafer from their sleeve and pressed it into Lio's hand.

"What is this?" he asked.

"The event log from yesterday. The risk directory. Today's scenario. Everything I copied."

"Why give it to me?"

"Because they are watching what I protect."

She closed his fingers around the wafer.

"They may be watching what you protect too."

The lift waited.

Rook stepped inside first. Quell followed. Senn stood beside the door.

Lio looked back at the operations floor.

The last assessment remained on the dark plot table in the afterimage of his mind.

**AUTHORITY UNRESOLVED.**

The doors began to close.

He stepped into the lift.

Before the seam sealed, Anik spoke from the room behind them.

"Tomorrow, we stop pretending the scenarios are hypothetical."
