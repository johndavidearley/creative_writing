# Black Banner Academy: Chapter 11 Draft

## Public Systems

The command vault did not stay secured for long.

The civilian inspectors had access to the evidence archive, but the yard commander still controlled the power grid. The fleet security craft still surrounded the repair ring. The private continuity channel still carried orders through systems nobody would admit to owning.

Rook remained above the outer ring with the maintenance tug.

Lio, Quell, and Senn stood inside the operations floor without academy access, watching their own names disappear from the command network one permission at a time.

**ARDEN, L. — COMMAND ACCESS REVOKED.**

**QUELL, A. — CIVILIAN INTERFACE REVOKED.**

**VALE, S. — SYSTEMS INTERFACE REVOKED.**

The private channel did not revoke them.

It still accepted their credentials.

Senn read the contradiction and gave a tired laugh.

"They removed us from the system they can be asked about."

"And kept us in the system they deny exists," Quell said.

Wren stood near the service lift. The red emergency lights had softened her face, but not the exhaustion beneath it.

"You should leave the operations floor," she said.

"We are not academy personnel anymore," Lio replied. "Where should we go?"

"Anywhere that is not here."

"The yard is still under an authority conflict."

"Civilian inspectors have the archive."

"They have the archive inside a vault controlled by a commander who has refused the warrant."

"They can preserve it."

"Can they?"

Wren looked at Senn.

Senn shook their head. "The archive is readable. It is not independently verified. The yard command system can claim the custody record was altered after the seizure."

"Then create an independent copy," Wren said.

"The public mirror is already running."

"The yard's emergency network is local. It will not survive a full system reset."

Quell turned. "You know they are preparing a reset."

"I know the yard commander has lost control of the public record and will try to regain control of the system."

"When?"

Wren checked the emergency display.

"Soon."

A new message arrived from the civilian inspector channel.

**ARCHIVE CONTENT CONFIRMED. REQUESTING EXTERNAL EVIDENCE RECEIVER.**

Lio opened the reply field.

"What is an external evidence receiver?" he asked.

Senn answered before Wren could.

"A system outside the yard that can verify the archive without accepting the yard's chain of custody."

"Do we have one?"

Quell looked at the civilian network. "The academy has one."

Wren's face changed. "No."

"The public review system mirrors all academy command exercises," Senn said. "It was built for post-action training review."

"It is not cleared for live operational data."

"That is why it can verify the archive. The mirror is administratively separate from fleet command."

"The academy will shut it down."

"They will have to admit it is running."

Wren moved toward Senn's console. "Do not route the yard archive through the academy."

Lio turned to her. "Why?"

"Because the academy is not a neutral receiver. It is the institution that allowed Black Banner to exist."

"Then it is also the institution that must be forced to see it."

Wren stared at him.

"You think visibility creates accountability," she said.

"I think secrecy has already created the opposite."

The public mirror appeared on Senn's display. It was an old academy interface designed in gray and blue, with a training emblem in the corner and a list of archived exercises below it.

The most recent entry was blank.

**LIVE REVIEW SESSION: UNAUTHORIZED SOURCE.**

Senn looked at Lio. "It can receive the archive, but it requires an instructor witness."

"Can you bypass that?"

"Yes."

"Do not."

She looked surprised.

"If we bypass it, they will say we corrupted the review system."

Quell stepped beside them. "We need a witness."

Wren's voice was quiet. "No instructor will sign this."

"One already has access," Lio said.

He looked at her.

Wren did not move.

"You brought us into the system," he said. "You can witness what the system did."

"I cannot authenticate the academy's review receiver."

"You can authenticate that the cadets were trained in the same systems now operating the yard."

"That is not the same thing."

"It is enough to establish continuity."

"And after that?"

"After that, the record belongs to everyone."

Wren looked toward the service lift. The door remained open, waiting for someone to choose a direction.

"You do not understand what happens when a record belongs to everyone," she said.

"Then tell us."

"It stops being evidence and becomes ammunition. Every person who sees it will use it to prove the thing they already believed. The fleet will call it sabotage. The ministry will call it an unauthorized military channel. The academy will call it a cadet breach. Black Banner will call it a necessary response."

"What will you call it?" Quell asked.

Wren's eyes moved to the live yard feed.

"A failure I helped make useful."

Nobody spoke.

The yard lost power again.

This time, the private continuity channel flickered with it.

The emergency displays remained alive for a few seconds, then shut down one by one. The command vault camera disappeared. The public traffic feeds went dark. Rook's voice broke into static.

Senn pulled the academy mirror closer.

"The yard is resetting the network."

"Can you keep the private channel?" Lio asked.

"For another minute. Maybe two."

The civilian inspector's message appeared in a narrow white box.

**ARCHIVE ACCESS LOST. EVIDENCE CUSTODY INTERRUPTED.**

Quell's hand closed around the edge of the console.

"They are erasing it."

"The archive is not gone," Senn said. "The public mirror received a partial copy."

"Partial is not enough."

"It is if it includes the access logs."

Lio looked at the mirror.

The received data showed the Black Banner training environments, the live traffic override, the continuity assignments, the detention protocols, and the private commands issued during the yard crisis.

It did not show who had created the program.

It showed enough to prove it existed.

A new incoming signal appeared. It did not come from the yard.

**CIVILIAN EMERGENCY CHANNEL: PRIORITY OVERSIGHT.**

The voice that followed was familiar.

"This is Iven Marr, civilian systems witness. I am requesting access to the academy public review mirror."

Lio stood very still.

"Iven?"

The voice continued through the emergency channel.

"I have an external evidence package concerning Advanced Integrated Studies, its funding history, and the suspension of Oversight Liaison Mera Sile. I am requesting a live receiver and an accountable witness."

Quell looked at Lio. "He is outside the yard."

"How did he get the channel?"

Senn's hands moved over the console. "The academy's civilian network."

Wren closed her eyes for a moment.

Iven's voice returned.

"If anyone is receiving this, identify the authority under which you are operating."

Lio opened the channel.

"This is Cadet Commander Lio Arden. We are operating under an unverified continuity channel at Kestrel Yard. Our academy command access has been revoked. Civilian traffic authority was exercised by Cadet Quell under disclosed emergency conditions. Systems access was exercised by Cadet Senn under recorded credentials. Pilot Rook Sato is maintaining the outer transfer ring."

Quell touched his arm.

He kept speaking.

"We are requesting the academy public review mirror as an external evidence receiver."

Iven was silent for half a second.

Then he said, "Request accepted."

Senn brought up the mirror's witness field.

**EXTERNAL CIVILIAN WITNESS: MARR, I.**

The system accepted his identity.

A second field opened.

**INSTRUCTOR WITNESS REQUIRED.**

Wren looked at it.

"No," she said.

The private channel came alive with Anik's voice.

**Do not complete the mirror.**

Lio did not answer.

**Arden, this is a direct order.**

The command appeared alongside the public witness field. Iven could see it. Quell could see it. Senn could see it.

Lio opened the public channel.

"The private command has not identified a public authority. We will not follow it."

Anik's voice lowered.

**Then I will identify the consequences.**

A file appeared on Lio's display.

It was Iven's profile.

The same risk directory from the laundry. The same clean sentences built from private history.

**SUBJECT: MARR, I.**

The first line described his family debt. The second described his access to civilian oversight. The third listed the people who depended on his recommendation for housing and food allocations.

The final entry had been updated today.

**SUBJECT WILL EXPOSE CHANNEL IF PERSONALLY THREATENED. USE DISCREDITING PROCEDURAL FAILURE AS PRIMARY RESPONSE.**

Iven's voice continued through the channel.

"I can see the file."

Lio felt the room tilt.

Anik had not threatened Iven directly. He had displayed the information where Iven could see that it had been prepared for him.

"You are listening," Anik said. "That is enough. Withdraw the mirror request and the file will remain private."

Quell moved toward the console.

"He is using the profile as a threat."

"He is showing us what he has."

"That is a threat."

Senn looked at Lio. "If we broadcast the file, we expose Iven's private information."

The choice had been designed well.

Protect Iven by keeping the evidence private.

Protect the evidence by making Iven part of the record.

Refuse both and let Black Banner decide what became visible.

Iven spoke through the channel.

"Lio, do not redact my file."

"Iven—"

"If you protect me by hiding what they have, they will use the same protection against everyone else."

"You do not know what the file contains."

"I know enough."

Anik's voice cut through. **Marr, you are not authorized to make that decision.**

Iven answered him.

"Neither are you."

The channel went silent.

Senn's hands hovered over the mirror controls.

"I can publish the command, the threat, and the profile as separate layers. The receiver will preserve the original source and mark the private data as restricted."

"Who can access it?" Quell asked.

"The civilian witness. The academy review board. Any external authority the witness shares it with. Not the public broadcast unless someone releases it."

"Then do it."

Lio looked at Quell.

She held his gaze.

"He has consented," she said. "Make the threat visible without making his private life public."

Senn began the transfer.

The academy mirror accepted the private command, the profile threat, and the access logs. The data moved in a narrow stream from the yard's private channel through the civilian witness receiver.

The instructor witness field remained empty.

Wren stepped toward the console.

"I will sign."

Lio looked at her.

"As what?"

"As the instructor who established the systems used in the response."

"Did you establish Black Banner?"

Her hand hovered over the witness field.

"I established the assessment architecture."

"That is not the same thing."

"It is enough to authenticate the training chain."

"Not the authority chain."

"No."

She entered her name.

**INSTRUCTOR WITNESS: WREN, T.**

The system asked for a statement.

Wren typed one line.

**I certify that these systems were used to train the cadets and were later connected to live operational networks without ordinary academy authorization.**

She submitted it.

The public review mirror opened.

The academy logo appeared at the top of the screen.

Then the evidence began to play.

The first record showed the Chapter Three training exercise, the live override, and the continuity acknowledgment. The second showed the risk profiles and Anik's offer to delete one file. The third showed the patron dinner invitation, the recognition pins, and the private access assignments. The fourth showed Iven's evidence and the prepared notice identifying him as an unsecured channel.

The fifth showed Kestrel Yard.

The civilian traffic collapse. The detention order. The hidden command. Lio's broadcast. Rook's refusal. Senn's archive seizure. Quell's public authority statement.

Every event carried a timestamp.

Every command carried a source.

The academy review system did not interpret the evidence. It displayed it.

That was enough.

The fleet channel returned first.

**ACADEMY REVIEW MIRROR IS AN UNAUTHORIZED OPERATIONAL BROADCAST. DISCONNECT IMMEDIATELY.**

The civilian witness channel responded.

"This is Iven Marr. The receiver is operating under civilian evidence custody. State the authority for disconnection."

No answer came.

The yard commander's channel opened.

"This is Commander Halvek, Kestrel Yard authority. I order the academy mirror disconnected."

Quell replied, "State the authority under which you are ordering the disconnection."

"I am the yard commander."

"Your emergency succession has not been entered into the civilian registry."

"The ministerial shuttle was destroyed."

"That establishes an emergency. It does not establish your authority to erase evidence."

Commander Halvek's voice became harder. "Cadet, you are interfering with a military operation."

"Yes," Quell said. "I am interfering with an unverified order to destroy a public record."

The statement entered the mirror as a new evidence line.

**QUELL, A. — AUTHORITY CHALLENGE RECORDED.**

Lio watched the live feed from the command vault. The contractor had disappeared. The civilian inspectors remained inside, copying the archive to external storage. The fleet security craft had moved away from the vault but not left the yard.

The public mirror continued.

The academy's system began to slow under the number of external requests.

Senn looked at the transfer graph. "We are getting too much traffic."

"Can the mirror handle it?"

"Not if they keep trying to shut it down."

"What happens if it fails?"

"The evidence already has external witnesses. But the live chain will break."

Iven's voice came through the channel.

"The civilian network is opening a secondary receiver."

Senn looked at the source. "Where?"

"The academy's old public archive."

"That system has been decommissioned."

"It has been marked decommissioned. It still responds to civic records requests."

Lio almost smiled.

"You found another door."

"I found a system that never learned how to forget."

Senn routed the mirror to the secondary receiver.

The evidence stream split.

The academy review mirror carried the live feed. The civic archive preserved the source records. The civilian witness receiver held the custody chain.

No single system controlled the evidence anymore.

Anik's voice returned.

It was no longer calm.

**Arden, terminate the broadcast.**

Lio opened the private channel.

"State your authority."

A pause.

**You know my authority.**

"I know your access."

**I brought you into this response. I made your systems work. I kept the yard alive.**

"You did not keep it accountable."

**Accountability is a luxury when people are dying.**

"Then why are you trying to stop the record now?"

No answer.

The broadcast continued.

Iven spoke over the civilian channel.

"This is a public evidence custody notice. The archive is being received by civilian, academy, and civic systems. No single operator may alter or delete the record without a witnessed authorization."

The words were procedural.

They sounded like a door locking.

The yard's power grid stabilized.

The civilian traffic ring began to clear.

The ministry shuttle's emergency beacon returned to the system, weak but active. It had not been destroyed. It had been forced into a blind transfer orbit and was now being guided toward a rescue dock.

For the first time, the crisis produced a fact that did not require interpretation.

The shuttle was alive.

Rook's voice came over the repeater. "They have three survivors from the shuttle. Maybe more."

Quell closed her eyes briefly.

Senn kept the evidence transfer running.

Lio watched the academy mirror fill with outside requests. Fleet officers. Civilian clerks. Academy instructors. Students who had found the public archive through ordinary channels.

The hidden program had spent weeks turning secrecy into belonging.

Now the record belonged to strangers.

A new message appeared on every receiver.

**LIVE EVIDENCE REVIEW: AUTHORITY CHAIN UNRESOLVED.**

Below it, the yard's public system added another line.

**ALL ORDERS SUBJECT TO REVIEW.**

Anik's private channel closed.

Wren stood beside Lio, watching the evidence continue without them.

"You have made this impossible to contain," she said.

"That was the point."

"No. The point was to keep the yard alive."

"Both things can be true."

Wren looked at the black pin on his jacket.

"You still wear it."

Lio touched the metal.

"I know what it means now."

"Do you?"

"It means someone saw what I could do and decided that was more important than who could review it."

Wren's expression broke at last.

Not into tears. Into recognition.

"Then take it off."

Lio looked at the public mirror, the civilian witness field, and Iven's name attached to the evidence custody chain.

He removed the pin.

The metal left a small indentation in the fabric of his jacket.

He placed it on the console beside Senn's hands.

The academy review system recorded the movement through a nearby camera.

Senn noticed.

"You know that will become evidence too."

"Yes."

The yard's emergency lights shifted from red to white.

Outside, the orbital repair rings turned slowly against the dark.

Inside, the evidence continued to move.

The question was no longer whether Black Banner had authority to act.

Everyone could see that it had acted without one.

The question was who would decide what happened next.
