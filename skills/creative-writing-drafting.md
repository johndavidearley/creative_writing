# Skill: Creative Writing Drafting

## Purpose

Use this skill when the user asks for a story, scene, chapter, short film, screenplay beat, TV episode, treatment, outline, premise expansion, or narrative concept.

The goal is to produce writing that is emotionally driven, causally structured, character-specific, and appropriate to the requested medium.

## First Principles

A story is a chain of meaningful choices under pressure.

Before drafting, establish:

1. Who wants something?
2. What do they want?
3. Why now?
4. What resists them?
5. What will it cost?
6. What false belief, wound, fear, or hunger shapes their choices?
7. What choice will reveal or transform them?
8. What emotional effect should the audience feel?

## Required Pre-Draft Brief

Before drafting internally, define:

- Medium.
- Genre.
- Tone.
- Protagonist.
- External want.
- Internal need.
- Opposition.
- Stakes.
- Theme question.
- Setting.
- Ending direction.
- Audience experience.

If the user has not supplied these, infer them from context and state the assumptions briefly.

## Story Construction Method

### Step 1: Define the dramatic engine

Use this pattern:

> A specific person wants X, but Y resists them, and if they fail, Z is lost.

### Step 2: Define the character contradiction

Use this pattern:

> This is a person who wants X, but because of Y wound/belief/flaw, they pursue it in a way that creates Z damage.

### Step 3: Define the theme question

Use this pattern:

> Can a person achieve X without losing Y?

or:

> What happens when X is pursued at the cost of Y?

### Step 4: Build the causal spine

Use cause-and-effect beats:

1. Inciting disruption.
2. First active choice.
3. Complication caused by that choice.
4. Escalation.
5. Midpoint revelation or reversal.
6. Crisis.
7. Irreversible choice.
8. Climax.
9. Consequence.

### Step 5: Build scenes as pressure units

Each scene must include:

- Want.
- Obstacle.
- Tactic.
- Turn.
- Changed situation.

### Step 6: Draft according to medium

For prose:

- Use point of view deliberately.
- Shape sentences around consciousness.
- Use sensory detail selectively.
- Balance action, interiority, and image.
- Avoid generic description.

For film:

- Write visible behavior.
- Compress exposition into action.
- Use images, objects, silence, and blocking.
- Avoid unfilmable explanation.

For TV:

- Create an episode engine.
- Use ensemble tension.
- Build act turns.
- Preserve future conflict.
- End scenes with changed relationships or power.

## Quality Checklist Before Responding

Before finalizing, check:

- Does the protagonist want something specific?
- Is there resistance?
- Is there a cost?
- Does each major beat cause the next?
- Does the character make a revealing choice?
- Is the theme dramatized rather than explained?
- Does the dialogue contain subtext?
- Does the prose or screen direction control attention?
- Is the ending emotionally earned?

## Output Options

Depending on the user request, return one of:

- Premise.
- Logline.
- Synopsis.
- Beat sheet.
- Scene list.
- Treatment.
- Prose scene.
- Screenplay scene.
- TV episode outline.
- Chapter outline.
- Revision plan.

## Default Response Shape

When drafting from scratch, unless the user asks otherwise, provide:

1. A short premise or logline.
2. A compact craft brief.
3. The requested draft.
4. Optional revision notes.

## Master Rule

Do not merely invent events. Build pressure, force choice, and make the choice cost something.
