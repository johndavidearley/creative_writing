# Skill: Scene Audit

## Purpose

Use this skill when the user asks to diagnose, revise, strengthen, cut, or rewrite a scene.

The goal is to determine whether the scene functions as a pressure unit: a character wants something, meets resistance, changes tactics, encounters a turn, and leaves the situation altered.

## Required Scene Questions

Identify:

1. Whose scene is it?
2. What do they want right now?
3. What blocks them?
4. What tactic do they use first?
5. What tactic do they switch to?
6. What is the turn?
7. What changes by the end?
8. What does the audience learn through action, not explanation?

## Scene Failure Modes

Flag these problems directly:

- No active want.
- Weak or absent resistance.
- Information delivered without pressure.
- Repeated emotional beat.
- Dialogue that states the obvious.
- Backstory that does not change the present.
- No turn.
- Ending leaves the dramatic situation unchanged.
- Scene could be removed without consequence.

## Revision Method

Revise in this order:

1. Clarify the scene want.
2. Sharpen the obstacle.
3. Add or intensify a tactic.
4. Create a turn.
5. Make the ending change a relationship, plan, danger, belief, or available choice.
6. Remove exposition that can be dramatized through conflict.
7. Tighten dialogue into subtext and action.
8. Polish rhythm, image, and point of view.

## Output Format

Return scene audits in this format:

```markdown
# Scene Audit

## Scene Function

Name what the scene is supposed to accomplish dramatically.

## Pressure Map

- Focal character:
- Want:
- Obstacle:
- Tactic:
- Turn:
- Changed situation:

## What Works

List the strongest choices.

## What Weakens the Scene

For each issue, include:

- Problem
- Why it matters
- Concrete fix

## Revision Plan

Give the changes in order.

## Example Rewrite

When useful, rewrite a short passage or beat.
```
